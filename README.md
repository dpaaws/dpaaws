[1991]  Linus Torvalds, 21 y.o student
        from Finland, posts on Usenet:

        "I'm doing a (free) OS...
         just a hobby. Won't be big."

        That hobby became Linux.

[1994]  Linux kernel 1.0 — released.
        The world changed quietly.

[2003]  Powers most of the internet.
        No announcements. Just uptime.

[NOW]   ~96% of top servers run Linux.

Linux is not just an OS — it's a philosophy. Built by a Finnish student as a weekend project, then handed to the world with no strings attached.

What followed was unprecedented: thousands of developers across the globe, no company, no CEO, no central authority — just code, trust, and a shared vision of openness.

Today Linux powers servers, supercomputers, satellites, smartphones, and the International Space Station. It is the backbone of the modern internet, and proof that open collaboration beats closed monopoly.

**Every developer owes Linux something.**
